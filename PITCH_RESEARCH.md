## 1 · Pitch-deck craft (all claims VERIFIED against primary sources)

### The canonical structures
- **Sequoia (10 sections, exact order):** Company purpose → Problem → Solution → Why now? → Market potential → Competition/alternatives → Business model → Team → Financials → Vision. Per-slide glosses verbatim: purpose = "define your company in a single declarative sentence"; vision = "If all goes well, what will you have built in five years?" Note: *Why now?* sits **fourth**, before market size; there is no dedicated traction slide. — [sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)
  - *Caution from the fact-check:* do **not** claim "Airbnb used this template" — Sequoia never says so; the mapping is retroactive analyst work.
- **YC seed (Aaron Harris, 9 sections):** Title → Problem → Solution → **Traction (4th)** → Why you're special → Business model → Market (7th) → Team → The Ask. Governing line: *"Focus on narrative. The rest is commentary."* — [ycombinator.com/library/2u](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/2u-how-to-build-your-seed-round-pitch-deck)
- **YC's slide-count rule is a *set* rule:** only the title must be exactly 1 slide; every other section is a set where n=1 is ideal and n>3 is discouraged ("You probably don't want any set here where n > 3. This is a seed deck, remember."). — [YC seed template (Google Slides)](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1wvlvQBOPtmIbN3I7onp--7X5RzEJFp_ajQlasrEagho/htmlpresent)
- **YC Series A:** 10–15 slides, built one-point-per-slide from a bulleted narrative outline ("fundraising vertebrae"); order front-loads proof — Title → Traction teaser → Problem → Solution → Traction in depth → Market → Competition → Vision → Team → Use of funds. Competition slide = a **moat** slide: "Show why you're 10x better than everyone else…" — [ycombinator.com/library/8d](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8d-how-to-build-a-great-series-a-pitch-and-deck)
- **Kawasaki 10/20/30, verbatim:** "a pitch should have ten slides, last no more than twenty minutes, and contain no font smaller than thirty points." — [guykawasaki.com](https://guykawasaki.com/the-only-10-slides-you-need-in-your-pitch/)
- **Slidebean's working structure:** 10–20 slides; an 18-slide max sequence starting Cover → Problem → Solution… — [slidebean.com/blog/pitch-deck-structure](https://slidebean.com/blog/pitch-deck-structure)

### What the reading data says (DocSend research, all verified/corrected)
- Average VC time per deck was **3:44 in the 2015 study**; by 2024 the weekly index ran ~**2:18–2:30**. Design for a 2–3-minute skim.
- **~19–20 pages, ~50 words per slide** is the working budget of real decks.
- Decks that **raised** were read ~4:10 vs ~1:36 for those that didn't (pre-seed 2020 report) — clarity earns time.
- **Long dwell on traction is a BAD sign**: VCs spent 80% *more* time on the traction of companies that failed to raise (confusion, not fascination).
- **"Why now?" scrutiny rose 65% YoY** in the 2022-23 seed data — successful decks foreground it.
- At pre-seed, **92% of successful decks had a Problem slide** (~1.8–1.9 pages); **only 59% had Traction** — pre-traction is normal at this stage.
- **No successful deck had a table of contents.** Funded decks placed Product and Why-now earlier.

### Famous-deck lessons (verified)
- **Airbnb** (~14 slides, $600K): Problem → Solution → Market validation → Market size → Product → Business model → Adoption → Competition (2×2 with an empty-quadrant claim) → Team.
- **Dropbox 2007** (17 slides, $1.2M, Sequoia): the benchmark for compression — problem in four words: *"Storage is a mess."*
- **Buffer** (13 slides, ~$500K): engineered around **one slide — traction** — because the founders had no other credential.
- **Front Series A** ($10M): the two slides investors called strongest were *"capital efficient"* and *"land and expand"* — not product, not market.
- **Intercom's first deck**: 8 slides, $600K. Less is viable.
- **Uber 2008** (25 slides): the cautionary teardown — strong *why now* (location tech + smartphones), but overlong and cluttered.
- **Hunter Walk on 2×2s:** "I'm going to ask you what the competitors on the slide would say about it." If you use a 2×2, be ready to defend both axes; a feature matrix fits checklist-evaluated categories.

> **The student is the inventory, not the customer.** Every incumbent monetizes someone else: Shiksha sells student contact details to colleges; CollegeDekho takes enrolment commissions; Unstop sells recruiter access to its student pool. — synthesis; anchor source [MediaNama on Shiksha](https://www.medianama.com/2026/05/223-choking-traffic-ai-dents-billings-shiksha-info-edge/)

## 3 · Global analogues (single-pass researched, sourced)

- **Common App:** 9.42M applications from 1.43M applicants through Mar 1 2026 (+5%/+2% YoY); avg **6.59 apps/applicant**; **1,100+ member colleges (closed, US-centric)**; college fees typically $30–75 (~$295 for 6 schools); **India applicants down 14% YoY**; Direct Admissions now at 213 institutions `[high/med]` — [commonapp.org deadline update](https://www.commonapp.org/files/DAR/deadline-updates/2025-26/)
- **Common App's fraud policy classifies substantive AI-generated content as application fraud** `[med]` — [commonapp.org/fraud-policy](https://www.commonapp.org/fraud-policy/) → *this defines the ethical line Scout must (and does) hold: assistive drafting grounded in the student's real record, human-owned final text, never auto-submitted.*
- **Handshake:** 25M users / 1,600 institutions / 1M employers; ~$435M raised at $3.3–3.5B (2022); by Apr 2026 ~$1.1B annualized gross revenue — **but almost all from AI data-labeling, not the job board** `[med]` — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/handshake-indeed-for-data-labelers/)
- **Simplify.jobs:** 1.5M+ candidates, claims 200M+ applications, autofill on 100+ ATSes; $39.99/mo premium; **accuracy degrades to 40–50% on legacy ATSes** `[high/low]` — [simplify.jobs/copilot](https://simplify.jobs/copilot)
- **LazyApply:** $99–999/yr volume tiers; **2.4/5 Trustpilot**; documented platform-ban risk (LinkedIn prohibits automation) `[high/med]` — the cautionary tale for spray-and-pray auto-apply.
- **Sonara:** shut down abruptly Feb 1 2024, locking users out of their data `[med]` — the risk of venture-fragile tools.
- **US counseling AI:** Kollegio ($2.8M seed, ~1M students) `[high]`; Nerd Apply ($3.2M for admissions-outcome data) `[high]`; CollegeVine (pivoted B2B); Empowerly ($30M total). US-only, counseling-only.
- **Scholarships:** Fastweb (1.5M scholarships, $3.4B listed, 9M members) `[high]`; Bold.org ($16.2M awarded counter) `[med]`.
- **The gap (synthesis):** *no major incumbent combines opportunity discovery with cross-platform application filling — each covers one half.*

## 4 · The pain, quantified (single-pass researched, sourced)

**India**
- **₹1,155 crore of ₹9,164 crore** earmarked for SC/OBC/EBC/DNT scholarships went **unused** in FY24-25 `[high]` — [Rajya Sabha reply via The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/caste/funds-for-scholarships-for-students-from-marg)
- Marginalised-student scholarship beneficiaries **collapsed ~60% in four years** (54.95L → 20.61L pre-matric OBC/EBC/DNT) `[high]`
- Seat math: **18 NEET registrants per MBBS seat** (22.79L vs 1.29L seats); **~27 JEE Main registrants per JoSAA seat** (16L vs 59,917) `[med]`
- Career counselling ratio ~**1 counsellor : 3,000 students** `[low]`; urban households already spend ₹9,950/yr/student on coaching `[high]`

**US (analogue market)**
- **$4.4B in Pell Grants unclaimed** by the class of 2024 (~830k eligible students never filed FAFSA) `[high]` — [NCAN](https://www.nasfaa.org/news-item/36182/)
- 40% of undergrad families used **no scholarships**; 71% of those **never applied**; top reasons are discovery & self-doubt (37% "didn't think there were scholarships for me", 41% "didn't think I'd win") `[high]` — [Sallie Mae, How America Pays 2025](https://www.salliemae.com/content/dam/slm/writtencontent/Research/HAP_2025.pdf)
- Discovery is fragmented across **6+ channels** `[high]`; typical applicant submits **6.8 applications** `[high]`; **73% report high/very-high stress** — most stressful part of senior year 4 years running `[high]` — [Princeton Review 2025](https://www.princetonreview.com/press/college-hopes-worries-press-release-2025)
- Time cost: est. **60–200 hours** per college application season (advisor estimates, not survey data) `[low]`
- **Myth to avoid:** "$100M in scholarships goes unclaimed" is debunked (Kantrowitz); the real unclaimed money is federal aid never applied for `[high]`

**Behaviour shift**
- Gen Z discovers opportunities on **social feeds, not job boards**: 76% rely on Instagram for career content vs 34% LinkedIn `[med]` — [Zety 2025]
- Internship postings on Handshake fell 15%+ (Jan'23→Jan'25) while applications surged `[high]`

## 5 · Why now (single-pass researched, sourced — the 3-force argument)

**Force 1 — the capability just crossed the line.**
- OSWorld 2024: best model completed **12.24%** of real computer tasks vs 72.36% human baseline `[high]`. By Jul 2026: top scores **78–85% — above the human baseline**. ~6x in two years. — [osworld-v1.xlang.ai](http://osworld-v1.xlang.ai/), [llm-stats leaderboard](https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/osworld-verified)
- Computer use is now a **built-in capability of mainline frontier models** (Gemini 3.5 Flash native at 78.4%; Claude Opus 4.8 at 84% Online-Mind2Web) `[high/med]`
- Clean-print document OCR effectively solved (94.6% OmniDocBench, "saturated") `[med]` — but **handwriting is NOT** (0.60 socOCRbench) `[high]` → matches Scout's design: vision-read printed marksheets, never promise handwriting.

**Force 2 — students already moved.**
- Teen AI use for schoolwork: **13% ('23) → 26% ('24) → 54% (late '25)** `[high]` — [Pew, Feb 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/)
- **46% of US high-schoolers used AI in their college search by late 2025 — doubled in six months** `[high]` — [EAB](https://eab.com/about/newsroom/press/ai-in-college-search-survey/)
- UK HE: **94% of students** use genAI for assessed work `[high]` — [HEPI 2026]
- Policy vacuum: **68% of colleges have no AI-essay policy**; 30% ban; 2% allow `[high]` — [Kaplan 2025]. Caltech: AI-drafted essays can rescind admission. Common App: substantive AI content = fraud.

**Force 3 — the incumbents' model is dying of AI.**
- Shiksha's billings −13% YoY *because AI answers ate its SEO traffic* `[high]`; upGrad buying distressed assets; CollegeDekho burning 57p/₹ on ads. The lead-gen era is ending; a student-side agent is the successor model.

**Counterweights (keep the deck honest):**
- Long-horizon agents still weak: best-in-world completes **20.6%** of long real-world workflows `[high]`; independent evals ~42% vs vendor claims `[high]`; **$5.26/task** cost benchmarks `[high]`; prompt injection unsolved (OpenAI: "unlikely to ever be fully solved") `[high]`; Gartner: >40% of agentic projects cancelled by 2027 `[med]`. → Scout's architecture already reflects this: form-filling on the *user's* click, never auto-submit, no arbitrary browsing, human review before send.
- Every flagship consumer browser-agent of 2024-25 (Operator, Mariner, ChatGPT Agent) was **shut down or absorbed** `[med]` → don't pitch "autonomous agent", pitch "the application copilot with hands".

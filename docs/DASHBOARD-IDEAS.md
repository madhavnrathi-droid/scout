# Scout Dashboard — Ideas to Make It Better

Plain-English ideas for growing the dashboard. Written so each one is easy to picture.

**How to read the tags:**
- 🟢 **Quick win** — mostly showing data Scout already has. Days, not weeks.
- 🔵 **Bet** — a real feature worth a sprint.
- 🟠 **Strategic** — shapes the product or the money. Decide on purpose.

**The one idea behind all of it:** students use Scout in bursts — hard during
admissions season, then quiet for weeks. So the dashboard should not try to
build a daily habit (the research says daily streaks actually backfire here).
It should be **impossible to miss when something matters**, and **quietly useful
when nothing's due**.

---

## The 4 biggest moves

**1. Make the season the reason to come back.**
Instead of a fake daily streak, show a calendar of the whole admissions season —
what opens, what closes, what to prep now. The calendar is the hook a streak
can't be, because it rises and falls with real deadlines.

**2. Show "reach / match / safety" on the home screen.**
Scout already calculates this and hides it in one tab. Picture three little bars:
"2 moonshots · 3 realistic · 0 safe bets" — with a warning when there are zero
safe options. It turns the scary question *"am I good enough?"* into a to-do list.

**3. Give the smart stuff away free; charge for the work.**
Every rival tracker hides its AI and analytics behind a paywall. Scout should do
the opposite — give away the odds and matching (that's the "we work for you"
flag) and charge for the **agent that actually fills the applications**. Real
time saved is worth paying for.

**4. Put "everything lives in your browser" where people see it.**
Scout's biggest trust advantage — no account, nothing uploaded, nobody sells
your number — is buried in settings. For students sick of ed-tech spam, that's a
headline, not a footnote.

## 1 · Getting students to come back

**S01 · Season calendar** 🔵
A timeline of the whole admissions cycle: what opens this week, what closes, what
needs prep now. This is the main reason to check back.

**S02 · Stretch the deadline strip to 90 days** 🟢
Today already shows a 21-day deadline bar. Make it a 3-month band with month
markers. Now Scout is where you *plan*, not just react.

**S03 · "What changed since you were away"** 🔵
A student back after two weeks sees just the delta: new strong matches, windows
that opened, deadlines that crept closer. Makes returning feel rewarded.

**S04 · Weekly digest, not daily pings** 🟠
One good Sunday message ("your week in applications") beats a daily nag. Research
is blunt: too many notifications and a third of users uninstall.

**S05 · "Nothing's due — here's 10 minutes well spent"** 🟢
When the to-do list is empty, offer one useful prep step: the weakest part of the
profile, an unwritten essay, an unread match. A dead visit becomes a useful one.

**S06 · Only nudge on real events** 🔵
A notification fires when a deadline enters the danger zone or a saved window
opens — never on a timer. Fewer, truer alerts.

---

## 2 · Motivation without the guilt trips

**S07 · A "readiness" bar, not a streak** 🟢
Show how complete a student's profile is as a progress ring. Unlike a streak, it
can't "break" — so it never punishes someone for being away.

**S08 · Let students set their own weekly goal** 🔵
"This week I want to send 2." Scout tracks it and celebrates hitting it — never
shames missing it. Self-set goals motivate; imposed ones feel pushy.

**S09 · A little "ready to send" ring on each application** 🟢
Each row shows how close it is: fields done, essay attached, documents ready.
100% means "hit send." This is what moves a draft to a real submission.

**S10 · Celebrate results, not logins** 🔵
When a student marks a win or a shortlist, give it a quiet, genuine moment. That
memory is what brings them back next season.

**S11 · A "you're on a roll" line** 🟠
"You've sent 4 this month — your best in 4 months." Pride, framed as a personal
best. Never a comparison to others.

---

## 3 · Numbers that actually help (not vanity)

*The rule: every number must change what the student does next. If it can't, cut it.*

**S12 · Reach / match / safety balance** 🔵
The most valuable thing Scout computes and never shows on the home. Warn loudly
when there are zero safe options. (This is move #2 above.)

**S13 · Odds on each application — free** 🟢
A grounded "how likely / how competitive" read on each listing, with the *why*.
Rivals charge for this; giving it away is Scout's clearest "we're different."

**S14 · Follow-through rate, front and center** 🟢
"You've sent 60% of what you saved." This is literally the problem Scout exists
to fix — saved-but-never-sent. Already calculated; just show it.

**S15 · A simple funnel with the leak marked** 🔵
Saved → drafting → sent → heard back, with the biggest drop-off called out:
"6 drafts stalled — want Scout to draft the next 3?"

**S16 · "You're missing a document" alert** 🟢
A missing certificate sinks an application for sure, where a near deadline is only
maybe. So "3 applications need a document you don't have" should rank *above*
deadline warnings.

**S17 · Hours spent & money on the table** 🟠
"You've put in ~22 hours" and "₹X in prizes riding on your live applications."
Makes the pipeline feel worth protecting. Already computed.

---

## 4 · Trust (the whole point)

**S18 · Show where each opportunity came from + how fresh** 🟢
"from Unstop · updated 12 min ago" on every card. Kills the "is this real?"
doubt. Scout already has both pieces of data.

**S19 · "Lives only in your browser" on the home** 🟢
No account, nothing uploaded, nobody sold your number — one quiet line where it's
actually seen. (Move #4 above.)

**S20 · Label every date: verified / expected / estimated** 🔵
Scout already does this for admissions. Extend it everywhere. An honest "we're
guessing" beats a confident wrong date.

**S21 · A visible "what Scout will never do" promise** 🟠
Name the things rivals do that Scout refuses — sell your number, auto-submit,
spam you. Turns the positioning into a promise students can hold you to.

**S22 · Keep the honest empty states** 🟢
Scout already shows helpful empty screens (ghost rows + a "try sample data"
button). That's a proven strength — protect it, and use the same pattern on
every new screen.

## 5 · Making money, honestly

**S23 · Free tracker, paid agent** 🟠
Keep the whole pipeline free. Charge for the agent that fills applications for
you. Free: watch it fill a few a month. Paid: unlimited + "fill all my ready
ones" in one go. Time saved is the pitch.

**S24 · A few free AI drafts, then unlimited** 🔵
Give, say, 5 free AI drafts/rewrites — enough to prove it's good — then paid.
It doesn't feel greedy because the free version genuinely works.

**S25 · Price for India: UPI, ~₹99–199/month** 🔵
The US apps charge $30–40. That doesn't fit here. UPI AutoPay costs almost nothing
to collect, so a low, student-sized price (coaching-class money) still works. One
tap, no card needed.

**S26 · Put "upgrade" on the home, not in settings** 🟢
Show the upgrade *at the moment it's earned* — right after the agent saves an
hour, or the 5th free draft lands. Not a nag buried in a menu.

**S27 · Give odds/matching away — as the flag** 🟠
It's the #1 thing rivals paywall, which is exactly why giving it free shouts "we
work for the student." Charge for the labour (S23), differentiate on the brains.

**S28 · Expect ~2.6% to pay — let the free feed drive growth** 🟠
Ed-tech converts low single digits. Don't build the product around paid; the free
discovery feed is the real growth engine. Paid is a bonus, never a hostage.

**S29 · Unlock features by referral, not just cash** 🔵
Students share more easily than they pay. "Invite 3 friends who finish a profile
→ a month of Plus." Fits the no-accounts model with a simple share link.

---

## 6 · Admissions — Scout's superpower

**S30 · Handle multi-round counselling (JoSAA etc.) properly** 🔵
These run several rounds where missing one day permanently costs a seat. Show them
as locked, in-order steps, with the *next irreversible deadline* pinned to Today.
Getting a student through this is life-changing.

**S31 · Put the reach ladder on the home** 🔵
The moonshot → match → safer list is Scout's signature admissions idea, stuck in
one tab. On the home it becomes a daily sanity check.

**S32 · Track fees & funding per application** 🔵
For abroad/scholarship applicants the worry is money and family. Add a funding
status column: fee due · waiver applied · scholarship attached · unfunded. Then
"4 applications with no funding plan yet."

**S33 · A one-page summary to show a parent** 🟠
Admissions are a family decision. A clean, human-readable printout — where I
applied, what it costs, what I'm waiting on — extends Scout to whoever's paying.

---

## 7 · The apply agent, scaled up

**S34 · An "applications in flight" board** 🔵
A card per application the agent is working on: planning · filling · your turn to
submit. Makes the agent's work visible and repeatable.

**S35 · "Fill all my ready ones" — the paid headline** 🟠
Point Scout at your 5 ready drafts and watch it fill each portal in turn, still
stopping at the submit button. This is the single most compelling reason to pay.

**S36 · Show the agent's track record** 🟢
"Scout has filled 47 fields across 4 applications for you." Honest proof of value
that earns the upgrade without a hard sell.

---

## 8 · Small things that raise the floor

**S37 · A ⌘K quick-jump for power users** 🟢
Type to jump to any opportunity, section, or action. Rewards heavy users, invisible
to newcomers.

**S38 · Work honestly offline** 🟢
Indian data is patchy. On a train, show "your saved data · feed offline" instead
of broken empty widgets.

**S39 · A plain-language pass on all the words** 🟠
"Dossier" already got renamed for being too fancy. The audience runs from 15 to
25 across languages. Simpler words widen the top of the funnel. (Hindi/regional
later.)

**S40 · One honest number in the browser tab** 🟢
Show "(2) Scout · closes today" in the tab title. Pulls a distracted student back
to a real deadline — no notification permission needed.

## What NOT to build (the research is clear)

- **❌ Daily streaks.** The big one. Broken streaks *reduce* engagement, and they
  "feel forced" on seasonal apps — job-search is named a bad fit outright. Scout's
  bursty rhythm makes a streak punish the exact student it's trying to keep.
- **❌ Streak "insurance" / anxiety-selling.** Charging to protect a streak profits
  when the user is anxious. That's the opposite of "the student is the customer."
- **❌ Leaderboards / ranking students against each other.** Risks crushing the
  weaker students who most need encouragement, in an already high-stress moment.
- **❌ Lots of notifications.** ~1 in 3 users uninstall over spam. Every alert must
  earn its place. Fewer, truer, event-triggered.
- **❌ Vanity numbers.** Any figure that doesn't change what a student does next is
  clutter. Scout's "every tile is a clickable filter" rule already enforces this —
  keep it.

---

## A few notes on the evidence

- The strongest, best-sourced findings: keep the tracker free and charge for the
  intelligence/agent; daily streaks backfire on seasonal apps (a 7-study paper in
  the *Journal of Consumer Research*, 2023); over-notifying drives uninstalls;
  showing a source + "last updated" removes the trust doubt.
- **Weaker / India-specific gaps:** exact Indian price points and conversion rates
  are thin — treat the ₹99–199 and ~2.6% figures as directional, not gospel. The
  cheap-to-collect UPI AutoPay point is solid.
- Most of the "quick wins" here aren't new features at all — they're **showing
  data Scout already calculates** in a place where it changes a decision.
